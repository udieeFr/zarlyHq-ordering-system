# Product

## Register

product

## Users

Sales administrators and managers at Zarly BigFood SDN BHD. Operational frontline staff working in an office or at the counter, under fluorescent light, on a desktop browser. They process orders throughout the day, approve payments, handle complaints, and generate digitally-signed documents. Speed and clarity matter more than aesthetics — a screen full of pending orders needs to communicate status instantly. Managers additionally track revenue, analytics, and audit trails.

## Product Purpose

ZarlyHQ is a signature-based online ordering management system. It replaces informal order intake (WhatsApp, Instagram DMs, verbal agreements) with a centralized, tamper-proof, legally defensible platform. Every order is cryptographically signed with Zarly's private key; SHA-256 hashes verify integrity after the fact. The system covers the full order lifecycle: customer placement, OTP verification, sales admin review, digital signature generation, payment verification, and delivery tracking. Success means staff can process orders confidently, audit trails stay clean, and every transaction has a signed PDF.

## Brand Personality

Professional, reliable, warm. The brand is anchored in orange (#ff9933) — assertive but not aggressive, confident but not cold. The tone is direct and practical. No fluff, no decoration that doesn't earn its place. Think: a well-run Malaysian business that takes its paperwork seriously.

## Anti-references

- Generic Bootstrap admin: pale grey sidebars, default blue buttons, table-heavy layouts with no visual hierarchy. The default look screams "student project."
- Heavy enterprise SaaS (SAP, Oracle): cramped density, every pixel used, navigation that requires a manual. Not suited to a team of a few operational staff.
- Consumer food delivery apps (Grab, Foodpanda): bright illustrations, playful tone, large tap targets. This is an ops tool — staff are processing, not ordering.

## Design Principles

1. **Status at a glance.** Any order table must communicate what needs action without requiring the user to read. Color, weight, and position carry status before text does.
2. **Warm authority.** Orange is the signal color. It means "attention here" or "primary action" — not decoration. Every orange element should earn that place.
3. **Precision in weight.** The system issues legal documents. The UI should feel deliberate: consistent spacing, sharp alignment, nothing approximate. Sloppiness in the interface undermines trust in the documents.
4. **Operational rhythm over showcase.** Frequent actions (approve, reject, review) must be effortless. No extra steps, no confirmation theater for low-stakes operations. Reserve modals and confirmation prompts for destructive or irreversible actions.
5. **Staff-first density.** More information per screen is a feature, not a flaw — if it's organized. Dense tables are correct here. Whitespace is used for hierarchy, not as a default.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Internal operational tool. WCAG AA contrast as a baseline (4.5:1 for body text). No special accommodation requirements specified. All interactive elements must be keyboard accessible for power users who tab through forms.
